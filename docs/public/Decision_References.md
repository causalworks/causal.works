# Decision References

A running log of the research and rules behind product decisions. Keep each entry to a line or two: what we decided, the practice or requirement that informed it, and a link where there is one. Best and required practices go here; do not name other apps or vendors anywhere; say "comparable tools". Full reasoning belongs in the dated plan file in `.claude/plans/`, which each entry can point to.

Add new entries at the top of their section. Format: **Decision** — what the source says. [link]. *Plan:* file name.

## Contacts and people

- **Every module points at the one contact record; no module keeps its own copy of a person.** Gifts, bills, invoices, grants, ledger entries and (later) dues all link to the same contact, and what a contact is to the organization (donor, vendor, customer, board member) is a role row, not a separate record. Comparable donor-management tools and the party model use one record per person or organization; a fixed customer-or-vendor type forces duplicates for someone who is both. Applied 2026-10-11 to gifts that arrive in the books: the donor on a suggested gift is picked from Contacts (adding a new contact there if needed), confirming gives that contact the donor role, and the ledger entry's contact is filled in when it had none, so Accounting and Funding show the same person. Research notes 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-06. *Plan:* 2026-10-04-crm-centered-architecture-discussion.md
- **Who paid and who is credited are separate links to contacts, and may differ.** An estate paying a deceased donor's pledge, a donor-advised fund, a foundation or an employer paying on a donor's behalf: the ledger entry keeps the payer, the gift credits the donor, and matching a receipt to a pledge does not require the two to be the same person. Our design, from Guy's example; the usual donor-database pattern of a soft credit is kept for showing credit to a second contact.
- **A gift counts toward giving totals unless it is void or dismissed, in every total.** One rule shared by the donor list, the donor's lifetime giving, the giving summary and top donors, and sponsored-project revenue, so a voided or "not a gift" entry cannot count in one place and not another.
- **Four kinds of "member" get four names (decided 2026-10-06):** cooperative org, dues member, staff / board member, workspace user. The bare word "member" meant all four across tables and screens. Naming follows the usual nonprofit-CRM habit of separating *why a record is in the system* (constituent codes such as Board member or Vendor) from *what the login may do*. Glossary lives in CLAUDE.md. *Plan:* 2026-10-06-contact-linkage-audit.md

## Board access and visibility

- **Board members see budget vs. actual, board report, 990 Part IX, grants and budget detail, read-only, at any time.** Boards are expected to get the balance sheet, statement of activities, budget vs. actual, cash flow, audit results and the 990 regularly. [Council of Nonprofits](https://www.councilofnonprofits.org/tools-resources/financial-literacy-nonprofit-boards), [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2017/07/21/financial-basics-for-nonprofit-boards/26206/).
- **Board access to financial records is a legal right, limited to what relates to their duties.** State attorney-general guidance: directors need basic financial and operating data to meet their duty of care. [Idaho AG](https://www.ag.idaho.gov/content/uploads/2026/07/Service-on-a-Nonprofit-Board.pdf), [Oregon DOJ](https://www.doj.state.or.us/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/guide-nonprofit-board-service.pdf). *Plan:* 2026-10-04-temporary-accounting-access-scope.md
- **Donor details need a per-person permission; boards get reports, not raw records.** Donor information is restricted to people who need it for their role; limited reports or dashboards are the usual way to serve a board.
- **Key-staff compensation is shared temporarily on request, not always available (decided 2026-10-04).** Sources list key-staff pay as board information; we treat it as sensitive and use the same 30-day renewable permission as other extras. Schedule J stays a work paper for finance and the accountant or auditor. Not built yet: a permission for this. Open: whether it is a new permission or part of an existing one.

## Temporary and per-person access

- **Settings sit with the module that owns the behavior, at three scopes: person, organization, and module or project (decided 2026-10-09).** Comparable products separate personal, workspace or team, and project settings, reach project settings from the project, and let a higher level set a default or a limit. Full design in [Settings_and_Permissions.md](Settings_and_Permissions.md).
- **Two admins are encouraged for any organization with more than one person.** A second admin removes the single point of failure: only an admin can reset another admin's password, and common administrator guidance is two to four admins, no more. Nonprofit guidance: no single point of dependency on one leader, and no shared logins. No formal nonprofit standard found.
- **Managers: per-person, per-area permission to change settings, same time-limited model as read-only access.** Keeps a bookkeeper or coordinator from needing a broader role, and keeps the grant logged and self-ending. See [Settings_and_Permissions.md](Settings_and_Permissions.md).
- **Access is per person, ongoing or 30 days renewable, no other durations.** Time-bound, auto-expiring access is standard security practice because standing access is rarely revoked. [Tenable on just-in-time access](https://www.tenable.com/cybersecurity-guide/learn/just-in-time-access-jit), [CrowdStrike](https://crowdstrike.com/en-au/cybersecurity-101/identity-protection/just-in-time-access). *Plan:* 2026-10-04-temporary-accounting-access-scope.md
- **A read-only role for board or finance committee members.** Internal-control guidance recommends a user who can view and print reports but not change anything.

## Accounting entry and controls

- **Admins can only post entries when they switch it on, and finance is told.** Keeps entry with the finance role as the default (separation of duties) while letting small orgs with no finance member work. *Plan:* 2026-10-04-admin-accounting-entry-switch-scope.md
- **Voids, not deletes, in the ledger; fiscal-year lock.** See the controls entries under Regulated activity.

## Gifts and pledges (decided 2026-10-11)

Accounting rules here are read from CPA-firm summaries and the FASB text; an accountant should confirm them for each organization. Where no source describes a practice, the entry says it is our design.

- **A pledge has a status, and the status decides whether it is booked: Intention, Conditional, or Unconditional.** A promise is conditional only when it has both a barrier (a match, a milestone, a measurable requirement) and a donor right of return or release; a conditional promise is not recognized until the condition is substantially met, and a statement of intent without a clear commitment is not recorded. [JMCo on ASU 2018-08](https://www.jmco.com/articles/nonprofit/conditional-contributions-asu-2018-08/), [FASB ASU 2018-08](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU_2018-08.pdf), [AAFRC](https://aafrc.org/what-is-a-conditional-contribution-for-a-nonprofit/). *Plan:* 2026-10-11-gift-intake-from-books-plan.md
- **An unconditional, documented multi-year pledge is booked when it is made, at present value,** not when the cash or an invoice arrives. Holding it back is the cash-basis treatment. Sources disagree on whether the discount rate is risk-free or risk-adjusted, so the rate is set by Accounting, with the auditor's view. [JMCo on pledge accounting](https://www.jmco.com/articles/nonprofit/pledge-accounting-for-nonprofits/), [AAFRC](https://aafrc.org/?p=326).
- **A payment on a booked pledge reduces the receivable; it is not new revenue.** So a booked pledge is not invoiced like a sale (that would count the revenue twice). We send a payment request that posts nothing; the only posting is the payment itself. [JMCo](https://www.jmco.com/articles/nonprofit/pledge-accounting-for-nonprofits/).
- **Each payment must be matched to its pledge right away.** An unmatched payment is counted as new revenue and double-counts. This is why the books recognize an expected pledge or gift when a receipt is entered.
- **One record per promise and one per payment, the payment linked to the promise, a review before anything posts, and a status you can see on each transaction.** Comparable tools post gifts to the books in reviewed batches, keep commitments and transactions apart and linked, and show a sync status on each transaction.
- **Funding marks a pledge Unconditional, and Accounting accepts it before it posts.** Same review step as above; the status change itself posts nothing. A gift suggested from an entry the books already posted waits for Funding to confirm. Our design: those systems all flow from fundraising to accounting, and none describes the books-first direction. *Plan:* 2026-10-11-gift-intake-from-books-plan.md
- **Budget can show promised income with an estimate discounted from the organization's own collection history.** Our design, no source; shown as a suggestion beside the revenue line, never posted.

## State requirements (decided 2026-10-11)

- **The organization's state is a required basic setting.** State rules differ in the places this app touches: charity registration, unemployment tax, workers' compensation, 1099 copies and sales tax. Each state note names its source and the date it was read, and the unemployment figures are updated every January. Started with New York, North Carolina and Louisiana. *Plan:* 2026-10-11-state-driven-setup-plan.md
- **New York charities file the CHAR500-C with the Attorney General's Charities Bureau.** Due the 15th day of the 5th month after year end; above $1,000,000 in support and revenue needs a CPA audit, $250,000 to $999,999 a review (reports due on or after July 1, 2021). Older forms use other cut-offs. [NY AG](https://ag.ny.gov/resources/organizations/charities-nonprofits-fundraisers/charities-annual-filing-char500), [CHAR500-C instructions](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/regulatory-documents/char500-Ci.pdf).
- **North Carolina exempts charities under $50,000 in contributions that pay no officers, with an annual financial review.** Above that, a license. The old $25,000 figure is out of date. [NC Secretary of State manual](https://www.sosnc.gov/manual/charities/page157), [G.S. 131F](https://www.ncleg.net/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/PDF/ByChapter/Chapter_131F.pdf).
- **Unemployment taxable wage base for 2026: New York $17,600, North Carolina $34,200, Louisiana $7,000.** New York and Louisiana let a 501(c)(3) reimburse the state for benefits actually paid instead of paying contributions (New York: elect within 30 days after the quarter liability begins; Louisiana: change back only after two calendar years, requested 30 days before the year). [NY DOL](https://dol.ny.gov/unemployment-insurance-rate-information), [NC DES](https://www.des.nc.gov/employers/tax-rate-information), [Louisiana Workforce Commission](https://www.laworks.net/unemploymentinsurance/ui_lainsurancetaxrates.asp).
- **Workers' compensation: New York and Louisiana require it for paid staff of nonprofits, with a narrow officer exception;** North Carolina requires it at three or more employees. The app shows the rule and a link; the rate per $100 of payroll comes from the insurer's quote, not a default. [NY WCB](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/coverage-requirements-wc/nonprofit-no-employees.jsp), [Louisiana Workforce Commission](https://www.laworks.net/FAQs/FAQ_WorkComp_EmployerCoverage.asp), [NC Nonprofits](https://ncnonprofits.org/node/24394) (secondary source for North Carolina).
- **State copies of 1099s differ:** North Carolina wants a copy only when NC tax was withheld (Form NC-3, electronic, January 31); Louisiana wants a copy of each 1099-NEC for work done in Louisiana or by a Louisiana resident (February 28; the federal combined program or the state's transmittal). [NC DOR](https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/withholding-tax/enc3-frequently-asked-questions), [Louisiana DOR RIB 23-006](https://dam.ldr.la.gov/lawspolicies/RIB%2023-006%20State%20Filing%20Requirements%20for%20IRS%20Form%201099-NEC.pdf).
- **Sales tax for nonprofits differs:** New York exempts at purchase with an exemption number from the Department of Taxation and Finance; North Carolina refunds tax paid, claimed twice a year (October 15 and April 15); Louisiana gives no general exemption. [NY Tax](https://tax.ny.gov/bus/st/exempt.htm), [NC DOR](https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/sales-and-use-tax/other-sales-and-use-tax-resources/nonprofit-sales-and-use-tax-information), [Louisiana DOR](https://revenue.louisiana.gov/tax-education-and-faqs/faqs/sales-tax/how-do-i-get-a-sales-tax-exempt-number-for-a-non-profit-organization/).

## Banking and displayed amounts (decided 2026-10-11)

- **Bank-line match suggestions rank amount first, then date, then name and reference.** Bank text rarely reads like the name an entry was made under; users of comparable tools report the same amount-first behavior and that names and references are unreliable.
- **A statement imported over an existing feed must not import a transaction twice, even when the wording differs.** An exact-match duplicate check imports a line that reads differently as new; ours also counts lines already on the account by date, amount and direction.
- **Statement import takes a CSV or a PDF, whichever the bank provides.** Some accounting tools also take OFX, QFX, QIF and QBO files; we left those out on purpose because most small banks provide CSV or PDF. A PDF is read only when it has a text layer, and the preview checks the lines against the statement's opening and closing balances.
- **Removing a bill payment returns its bank line to Reconcile, from the bill as well as the register,**. Closed bank months still block it. Decided by Guy after comparing.
- **Negative amounts show in brackets, and green and red amounts are an optional setting, off by default.** Accounting convention for brackets; the color choice is Guy's. No source researched for either.

## Regulated activity (Organizational app) — from migration notes

Taken from the rules cited in each migration's own comments. The migration is named so the original reasoning can be read. Federal-award, 990 and Pub. 463 citations were checked against current official text on 2026-10-04 (links below). Other entries (state law, retention, fiscal sponsorship, internal controls) are practice-based and not re-checked.

**Federal awards (2 CFR 200, Uniform Guidance)** — [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200)
- **Second approver only for transactions on federal-award grants; light-touch everywhere else.** 2 CFR 200.303 requires internal controls, so the same person cannot create and approve a federal-award transaction or bill. Ordinary transactions stay unrestricted so a one-admin org can still work. Migrations 199, 207.
- **SEFA fields follow the real 200.510(b) list** ([2 CFR 200.510](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.510)). Checked 2026-10-04: it names the pass-through entity and its identifying number, and separately the total provided to subrecipients from each program. Includes both the pass-through entity the org received money through and the amount the org passes on to its own subrecipients, which are different things. Migration 229.
- **Procurement tiers: micro-purchase ($15K), simplified acquisition ($15K–$350K), formal bid or proposal above that.** From [2 CFR 200.320](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.320), which points to the dollar figures in [2 CFR 200.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.1) and the FAR ([48 CFR 2.101](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-48/section-2.101)). **Updated in code 2026-10-07:** the FAR inflation rule (90 FR 41872, Aug 27 2025) raised the simplified acquisition threshold from $250K to $350K on Oct 1, 2025. Micro-purchase stays $15K. `server/organizational/routes/bills.js` now uses $350K. Migration 245.
- **Indirect costs: two de minimis eras and the MTDC base** ([2 CFR 200.414](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.414), [200.1 MTDC](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/section-200.1)). The Oct 2024 revision raised the de minimis rate from 10% to 15% of MTDC (current text: up to 15%) and raised the subaward exclusion threshold from $25,000 to $50,000 for awards made on or after Oct 1, 2024. Participant support costs are a commonly missed exclusion. Known limit: the subaward exclusion is per whole account, not per subaward. Migrations 249, 251.

**Form 990 and financial reporting**
- **Functional expenses split program / management / fundraising and must total 100%.** FASB ASU 2016-14 requires the functional presentation; Form 990 Part IX asks for the same three-way split. Stored in basis points to avoid rounding. Migration 084.
- **Schedule J needs average hours per week for each person.** Part I line 1a. Migration 245. Access decision: see Board access above.
- **Fixed assets use a real register with depreciation posted to the ledger,** replacing a name-matching guess on Schedule D. Non-cash expenses (depreciation, amortization) use an explicit flag for the cash-flow statement for the same reason. Migrations 233, 235.
- **Statistical accounts (clients served, meals) use an explicit flag so no dollar total can include them.** Migration 228.
- **Board-designated funds are a tag on ledger lines, like donor-restricted grants, not a separate balance.** The balance cannot drift from the ledger. Migration 237.

**Controls and audit trail**
- **Fiscal-year close is a reversible lock, enforced by the database, with a required reason to reopen.** Orgs often close before an audit or tax return is final, so reconciliation is advisory, not a precondition. Migration 164.
- **Manual journals require a reason, and entries are voided, not deleted.** Manual journals skip the bill, invoice and bank flows, so who did it and why must be answerable later. Migration 294.
- **Every org always keeps at least one admin,** enforced in the app and again by a database trigger so a future bulk tool cannot break it. Migrations 264, 266.
- **Tenant isolation is enforced by row-level security on every org table,** including documents, which hold board resolutions and IRS letters. Written with a Single Audit or SOC 2 review in mind. Migrations 137, 225.
- **Vendor tax IDs are encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and kept in a separate admin-only table,** so donor-facing code never sees them. Migrations 204, 208.

**Payments, reimbursements, gifts**
- **Expense claims are separate from vendor bills.** Insider reimbursement differs in kind from arm's-length vendor bills: the claimant is also the payee, IRS Pub. 463 treats them differently for 1099 purposes, and self-approval is a real risk. Owed-to-insiders gets its own liability account for related-party review. Migration 236. [IRS Pub. 463](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p463) (the $75 / lodging-every-night rule is confirmed there).
- **Receipts: federal accountable-plan rules require documentary evidence only for lodging and any single expense of $75 or more.** State law varies, so the threshold is configurable and defaults to strict ($0). Migration 238.
- **1099 relevance is a default per account, editable per bill line.** Not every purchase from a 1099-eligible vendor is reportable. Migration 204.
- **Quid-pro-quo invoice lines split the exchange value from the contribution portion.** Only the fair-market-value part is exchange revenue. Migration 211.
- **In-kind gifts are the only gift type that needs a ledger path;** cash reaches the books through bank coding. They land pending and are posted inside Accounting. Migration 247.

**Fiscal sponsorship**
- **Standard (pass-through) sponsorship tracks revenue received and net amounts disbursed, not the sponsee's itemized expenses,** which stay in the sponsee's books. Each project can use a different model. Migrations 226, 227. *Plan:* 2026-09-14-fiscal-sponsorship-core-compliance.md
- **Every disbursement needs a deliberate approval before it is real.** Without genuine review, the IRS could read it as the sponsor not controlling the funds, which undermines variance power. This does not require a different person to approve, which is a deliberate difference from the federal-award rule. Migration 230.

**Records**
- **Retention defaults follow common nonprofit practice:** governing documents, 990s, audits and board minutes kept permanently; conflict-of-interest disclosures at least 7 years. Migration 123.

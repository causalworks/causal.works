Systems

How planetary boundaries, structural turnarounds, and leverage points fit together.

How this works

Every action connects to a bigger picture. Three layers are shown together:

  1. Planetary boundaries — how close each limit is to being crossed.
  2. Turnarounds — the structural shifts that hold a limit or restore one already crossed.
  3. Leverage — how much impact an action is likely to have.

What you see

  • A decision closing this week links to the structural shift it feeds.
  • That shift links to how close the relevant planetary limit is to being crossed.
  • An action can carry weight across more than one turnaround, and each is tracked.
  • As limits shift and decisions open or close, the picture updates to show where strain is highest and where action still has leverage.

Why timing matters

  • Once something is built (a pipeline, a law, a plant), it becomes rooted and harder to undo.
  • Whether a decision is still open is weighed as much as what it is about, because that is where the leverage is.

This is an early version of the framework. As Workshop's systems-mapping work feeds back into it, the links between actions and shifts should get sharper.

What needs to change

The Earth4All research program identifies five structural transformations that, if achieved by 2035–2040, address the primary drivers of planetary boundary transgression. These turnarounds are interdependent — energy transition requires inequality reduction to be politically feasible; food transformation supports poverty elimination.

Note: The five turnarounds address seven of the nine boundaries directly. Biosphere integrity and novel entities — among the most severely transgressed — are not fully covered by the turnaround framework. Actions addressing biodiversity, chemical pollution, and ocean governance appear in the feed and matter. They just don't fit neatly into any single turnaround.
